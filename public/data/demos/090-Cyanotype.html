§ I — The Craft

Every plate passes through two hands and one star

No presses, no printers, no shortcuts — only chemistry, patience and daylight.

Hand-Coated Emulsion

Each sheet is brushed with iron-salt emulsion by lamplight, then dried in the dark. No two coatings ever flow the same — the brushstrokes remain part of the image.

Exposed by Sunlight

Specimens are composed at dawn and exposed as the sun climbs — every plate read by eye, timed by shadow, never by clockwork.

§ II — The Process

Coat, expose, and watch it bloom

From blank paper to deep Prussian blue in a single morning.

  1. water_dropStep 01 — Coat

    Brush by lamplight

    A glass rod drags fresh emulsion across damp cotton rag; the sheet rests in the darkroom until it turns a faint olive green.

  2. wb_sunnyStep 02 — Expose

    Lay the specimen

    Leaves and seedheads are arranged directly on the coated paper under glass. In the sun the exposed ground turns steel grey; the silhouettes stay white.

  3. ecoStep 03 — Rinse & Reveal

    Wash and watch

    A cold bath carries off the unexposed salts and the print blooms, in about a minute, from grey ghost to deep Prussian blue.

§ V — Enquiries

Questions, answered slowly

Is every print really made by hand?

Coated, composed, exposed and rinsed by hand in our studio — the only machine involved is the sun, and it keeps no copies. Every plate is a unique object.

Will the blue fade over time?

Cyanotype is among the most lightfast of photographic processes. Behind UV glass and away from damp, a plate deepens with age rather than fading — Atkins's 1843 algae are still vivid today.

Do plates come framed?

Plates ship mounted on archival backing, ready for any standard A4 or A5 frame. Add a walnut frame with museum glass at checkout — $65 per plate, $340 for the folio.

How do you ship something so delicate?

Each plate travels between greyboard and waxed blue paper in a rigid mailer, insured worldwide. Anything broken in transit is re-pressed free on the next clear morning.

Do you accept specimen commissions?

Gladly. Send a cutting, a bouquet or the memory of a garden — we press the specimen, expose it, and you receive a plate from the very plant that stood in your hand.

§ VI — The Proof List

Be first to the next pressing

Twice a season we release a small pressing of new plates. Proof-list letters go out two mornings before anything reaches the public shelf.
