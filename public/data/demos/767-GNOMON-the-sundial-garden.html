How the garden works

Three things happen every clear day, whether anyone is watching or not.

01

The blade stands

A granite gnomon rises from the dial face, angled to the garden's latitude so its edge runs parallel to the Earth's axis.

02

The sun writes

As the sun crosses the sky, the blade's shadow sweeps across engraved hour arcs, each one cut by hand into the stone.

03

You simply notice

There is nothing to set, wind, or charge. You arrive, you sit, and the garden tells you what the sun already knows.

Quiet questions

Everything visitors tend to ask before their first morning in the garden.

The gnomon is the upright blade of a sundial, the part that casts the shadow. Ours is a single piece of granite, three metres tall, set at the exact angle of the garden's latitude so it keeps honest solar time all year.

The shadow rests when the sun does, and the garden simply becomes a garden. Many members tell us the overcast mornings, with the hour arcs standing quiet, are their favorite time to visit.
