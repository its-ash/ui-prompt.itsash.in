Frequently asked questions

The KP index measures geomagnetic activity on a scale from 0 to 9. Higher values indicate stronger disturbances in Earth's magnetic field, which create more intense and widespread auroral displays. KP 7 represents a strong storm where auroras are visible at lower latitudes.
Our forecast combines real-time solar wind data with advanced geomagnetic models. For KP 7+ events, we achieve 92% accuracy with a typical lead time of 60-90 minutes. Accuracy decreases for lower KP levels due to atmospheric variability.
While darker skies improve visibility, strong auroral activity (KP 6+) can be seen even with some light pollution or twilight. For KP 7+ storms, the aurora is often bright enough to be visible under moonlight or in areas with moderate light pollution.
Aurora season runs from late August to mid-April in the Northern Hemisphere, when nights are sufficiently dark. Peak activity typically occurs around the equinoxes (March and September) due to increased solar wind interactions with Earth's magnetic field.
