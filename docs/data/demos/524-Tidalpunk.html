What we build

Three currents, one grid

Every project pairs proven marine hardware with the communities that maintain it.

bolt

Tidal energy arrays

Slow-turning turbines anchored in predictable channels. They generate on every tide, in any weather, with no fuel and no noise above the waterline.

grass

Regenerative kelp farms

Long-line kelp beds absorb carbon, shelter juvenile fish, and supply local growers with food, fertilizer, and bioplastic feedstock each harvest.

recycling

Salvaged materials

Retired hulls and dock steel become turbine housings, reef structures, and workshop stock. Nothing seaworthy goes to the scrapyard.

From survey to first light

A cooperative project moves through three clear phases, usually within one year.

01 explore

Chart the site

We map currents, seabed, and seasonal life with local fishers before a single anchor drops.

02 construction

Build with the crew

Members assemble turbines and kelp lines in the harbor workshop from certified salvaged stock.

03 monitoring

Harvest and share

Power flows to the town grid at parity rates, and revenue returns to the cooperative ledger.

Questions from the dock

Do tidal turbines harm marine life?expand_more
Our rotors turn at under 12 RPM, slower than most swimming fish. Independent surveys at every site track mammal and fish activity, and arrays pause during migration windows.
What happens when the tide is slack?expand_more
Tides are predictable decades ahead, so slack periods are scheduled. Shore batteries charged at peak flow cover the short gaps, and the grid never notices.
Who owns the equipment?expand_more
The cooperative does. Members hold shares, vote on expansion, and receive dividends from power sales and kelp harvests each quarter.
Can I join without boating experience?expand_more
Yes. Most members start in the harbor workshop or on kelp lines. Certified trainers run free courses in rigging, diving safety, and turbine maintenance.
How is salvaged material certified safe?expand_more
Every beam and plate is ultrasonically tested and stamped before reuse. Anything that fails inspection becomes artificial reef substrate instead of structure.
