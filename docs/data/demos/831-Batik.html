From plain cloth to heirloom

A single tulis piece passes through three unhurried stages before it is ready to wear.

01

Draw the Wax

The artisan pipes molten wax along the pattern, sealing the cloth wherever the dye must not reach.

02

Crackle & Dye

The waxed cloth is dipped into the indigo vat. Fine cracks in the wax let veins of blue into the design.

03

Boil & Reveal

Hot water lifts the wax away, revealing the full motif. The cloth is washed, dried, and pressed by hand.

Questions from the vat side

What buyers most often ask before their first cloth.

How should I wash a hand-drawn batik cloth?chevron_right

Hand wash in cool water with a mild soap, then dry in shade. Natural indigo softens with age; avoid bleach and long sun exposure.

Why are there fine lines inside the pattern?chevron_right

Those are crackle veins, formed when dye seeps through hairline cracks in the wax. They are the signature of true tulis batik, not a flaw.

How long does a commission take?chevron_right

Most commissions take six to eight weeks, depending on motif density and how many dye rounds your chosen depth of indigo requires.

Do you ship internationally?chevron_right

Yes. Every cloth ships rolled in acid-free paper with tracking, and duties are calculated at checkout for most destinations.

Can I visit the studio or join a workshop?chevron_right

We host small workshops most Saturdays at our Yogyakarta studio. Book through the workshop link and we will confirm your bench by email.
