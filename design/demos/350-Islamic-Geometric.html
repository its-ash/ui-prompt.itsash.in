FAQ

Questions, Answered Plainly

What is girih?expand_more

Girih — Persian for “knot” — is the repertoire of star-and-polygon patterns composed from a small set of tiles, used across the Islamic world from the 9th century onward to ornament mosques, shrines, and manuscripts with mathematically precise symmetry.

Are the scans public domain?expand_more

The medieval works themselves are in the public domain. Our photography of the open collection is released CC0, and every record states its rights, photographer, and source monument — so you always know exactly what you may reuse.

Can I use the patterns commercially?expand_more

Yes. Anything marked CC0 needs no license or attribution. Scholar and Institution plans add a commercial license covering GIRIH’s contemporary photography, 3D scans, and Studio reconstructions.

Do you offer student discounts?expand_more

Yes — students and teachers receive 40% off any paid plan. Write to us from a valid academic address and we will send a code within a day.

How is the Studio different from other design tools?expand_more

Most tools draw shapes; the Studio reconstructs method. Every pattern carries its compass-and-straightedge construction, its symmetry group, and its historical source — so you learn how the masters composed it, not just what it looks like.

How does institutional licensing work?expand_more

Museums, universities, and practices can license the full archive for teaching, exhibitions, and research under a single annual agreement, with dedicated seats, invoicing, and priority support. Email our licensing desk for terms.
