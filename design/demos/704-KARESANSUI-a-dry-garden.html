Karesansui is a focus practice built on the Japanese dry garden. Each morning you clear distraction and place three intentions for the day.

Fewer features. More stillness.

Three tools, each designed to step out of the way once you begin.

The morning rake

Two minutes of guided raking across the sand. Each stroke clears a notification, a tab, a worry. When the sand is smooth, the day begins.

Three stones

Name up to three intentions. They stay in your garden until you lift them, never as a list of overdue tasks.

Quiet hours

Karesansui mutes every other app while you sit, then returns them gently when the bell sounds.

A practice in three breaths

  1. 一

    Rake

    Trace slow lines across the sand. Ten strokes, then the screen settles.

  2. 二

    Place

    Set down three stones for the day. Fewer is allowed. More is not.

  3. 三

    Sit

    Stay with the garden for five, ten or twenty minutes, with a single bell to close.

Gentle questions

Do I need to know meditation?

No. The garden is the guide. Most people simply rake, place and sit without any prior practice.

What happens to my stones?

They stay on your device, encrypted. Lift a stone when the intention is done and it quietly returns to the sand.

Will it send me reminders?

One, at a time you choose, and never more. Karesansui is designed to be the quietest app on your phone.

Can I cancel anytime?

Yes, in two taps. Your garden stays, returning to the free Pebble plan.

A small garden, delivered weekly

One short letter each Sunday: a raking pattern, a seasonal stone, and nothing else.
