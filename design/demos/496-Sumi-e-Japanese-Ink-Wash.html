The craft

Tools shaped by restraint

Three instruments, each drawn from the sumi-e tradition, each doing one thing well.

ink_pen

Living ink brush

Pressure-sensitive strokes that bleed, pool, and fade like ground sumi on rice paper. Every line is unrepeatable.

self_improvement

Breath-paced sessions

A soft cadence timer opens each sitting with one minute of stillness, so the hand settles before the brush moves.

auto_stories

The bound archive

Entries gather into seasonal scrolls you can revisit, export, or print as a hand-bound volume at year's end.

The practice

Three movements, daily

A sitting takes ten minutes. The ritual is the point.

01 water_drop

Grind the ink

Open a blank sheet. The studio dims, the inkstone turns, and your page waits without judgment.

02 gesture

Make the stroke

Write or draw one honest thought. The brush records pressure and pause, not perfection.

03 stylus_note

Seal the entry

Press your personal hanko beside the date. The entry dries, locks, and joins your archive.

Questions

Asked at the inkstone

Do I need a stylus to use the brush?expand_more

No. A finger or mouse works well. A pressure-sensitive stylus simply gives the ink more range, the way a softer brush would.

Can I export my journal?expand_more

Yes. Every scroll exports as a high-resolution PDF or image set, formatted like a folded album. Your words belong to you.

What happens if I miss a day?expand_more

Nothing. There are no streaks and no reminders. The blank page waits, and so does the ink.
