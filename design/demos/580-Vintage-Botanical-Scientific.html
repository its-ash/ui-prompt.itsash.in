Plate III., Method

The collector's method, in three movements

Modus operandi, observed in the field and at the writing desk.

I.

Gather

Photograph or describe your specimen where it grows; the registry notes place and hour on its own.

II.

Annotate

Add measurements, comparisons, and sketches. Cross-reference against plates from earlier seasons.

III.

Preserve

Bind entries into volumes, share plates with fellow naturalists, and keep the archive for posterity.

Plate VI., Enquiries

Questions commonly put to the curator

Quaestiones frequentes, answered without ceremony.

Must I know botany to keep a herbarium here?add

Not at all. The registry suggests classifications as you describe a plant, and the annotation guides assume curiosity rather than training.

Can I import an existing paper collection?add

Yes. Photograph each sheet and the registry will transcribe labels, dates, and localities into entries you may then correct by hand.

What becomes of my archive if I withdraw?add

Your specimens remain yours. Export the complete archive, plates included, as a bound folio at any time, subscription or no.
