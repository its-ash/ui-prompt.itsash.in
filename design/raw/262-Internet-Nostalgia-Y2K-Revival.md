## Historical Context

The early web was ugly and nobody cared. That was the point. GeoCities pages with tiled backgrounds, hit counters, "under construction" GIFs, and Comic Sans used unironically — it was a frontier built by amateurs who were genuinely excited about hyperlinks. Windows 95 gave an entire generation their first taste of personal computing through a beige box that felt like magic. The visual language was clunky system fonts, beveled buttons, 16-color palettes, and dialog boxes that asked if you were really, truly sure. Y2K revival isn't about faithfully recreating that era — it's about channeling the energy. The optimism of dial-up, the chaos of early HTML, the democratized ugliness before design became professionalized. Around 2018-2020, designers started pulling these references back not as mockery but as genuine aesthetic choice. Brutalist web design cracked the door open, and Y2K nostalgia kicked it down. Brands realized that millennials don't just remember this era — they miss the feeling of a web that wasn't optimized, surveilled, and A/B tested into sterility.

## When to Use

When your audience grew up clearing their browser cache to free up disk space. Millennial-targeted brands, retro tech products, meme-native companies, and anything that needs to signal "we don't take ourselves too seriously." Works brilliantly for limited drops, nostalgic product lines, or any brand that wants to feel human and imperfect rather than polished and corporate. Avoid if your users need to actually complete complex tasks — nostalgia is a vibe, not a usability framework.

## Design Principles

- System UI aesthetics over custom polish — use pixel fonts, beveled borders, window chrome, and OS-native UI patterns from Windows 95/98/XP as structural elements, not just decoration
- Embrace visible construction — show the seams with tiled backgrounds, table-based visual grids, visible borders, and elements that look hand-placed rather than algorithmically aligned
- Limit your palette to era-appropriate constraints — web-safe colors, dithered gradients, 1-bit icons, and the specific teals, purples, and silvers that defined Y2K futurism
- Motion should feel mechanical, not fluid — cursor trails, marquee scrolls, page transition wipes, and hover states that snap rather than ease. Nothing should feel like it was touched by a physics engine
- Layer irony with sincerity — the best Y2K revival work knows exactly what it's referencing and treats it with love, not condescension. A hit counter that actually counts is funnier and more committed than one that's obviously fake

## Components

- **Primary Button:** Generously rounded (1.5rem) shape. Accent color fill. Hover: 8% darken + subtle lift shadow. Active: -1px translate tactile press. Font weight 600. No outer glows.
- **Secondary / Ghost Button:** Outline variant. 1.5px border in muted color. Text in primary color. Hover: subtle background fill.
- **Cards:** Generously rounded (1.5rem) corners. Surface background. Subtle shadow (0 2px 12px rgba(0,0,0,0.06)). 1px border stroke.
- **Inputs:** Label above input. 1px border stroke. Focus ring: 2px accent color offset 2px. Error text below in semantic red. No floating labels.
- **Navigation:** Primary surface background. Active item: accent color indicator. Font weight 500 when active.
- **Skeletons:** Shimmer animation matching component dimensions. No circular spinners.
- **Empty States:** Icon-based composition with descriptive text and action button.

- No pure black (#000000) — use off-black or charcoal variants
- No oversaturated accent colors (saturation cap: 80%)

- No `h-screen` — use `min-h-[100dvh]`
