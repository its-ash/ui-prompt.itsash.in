## Historical Context

Golden amber carries centuries of European craft memory — from the Baltic amber trade routes that defined medieval commerce to the warm lacquer finishes on French ormolu furniture. It's a color that whispers old money without shouting it. When Mistral emerged from Paris as a serious contender in the AI race, their visual identity leaned into this exact territory: warmth over the cold blues of Silicon Valley, sophistication over sterility. The choice wasn't accidental. French design has always understood that technology doesn't need to feel clinical. The amber-gold spectrum sits at the intersection of intellectual rigor and sensory pleasure — think gilded manuscript illuminations, the patina on a brass astrolabe, afternoon light through Haussmann windows. It signals that intelligence can be beautiful, that precision and warmth aren't opposites. For AI brands specifically, golden amber solves a real problem: it humanizes. While competitors default to electric blue or void-black to signal 'advanced technology,' amber grounds the product in something tactile and historical. It says 'we built something powerful, and we're not afraid to make it feel approachable.'

## When to Use

Deploy Mistral Golden Amber when your AI product needs to feel European, premium, and distinctly non-Silicon Valley. It works beautifully for onboarding flows where you need to reduce intimidation, for pricing pages where warmth builds trust, and for brand moments where you want to signal craftsmanship over raw compute. Particularly effective for B2B AI tools targeting creative professionals, legal tech, and enterprise clients who associate blue-tinted interfaces with commodity software. Avoid it for developer-facing dashboards where amber might read as warning states.

## Design Principles

- Warmth as differentiation — use amber to deliberately contrast the cold palette conventions of the AI industry, making technology feel handcrafted rather than manufactured
- Restrained opulence — golden tones should suggest quality through subtlety, never crossing into gaudy territory; think matte gold leaf, not Vegas signage
- Functional hierarchy through temperature — reserve the warmest amber saturations for primary actions and trust signals, cooling toward neutral as elements become secondary
- Cultural specificity over generic futurism — let the European heritage inform typography pairings, spacing rhythms, and illustration style; this palette demands serif confidence
- Amber as semantic anchor — establish golden amber as your product's signature moment color, appearing at key emotional beats rather than flooding every surface

- **Secondary / Ghost Button:** Outline variant. 1.5px border in muted color. Text in primary color. Hover: subtle background fill.
- **Cards:** Sharp edges (0px) corners. Surface background. Subtle shadow (0 2px 12px rgba(0,0,0,0.06)). 1px border stroke.
- **Inputs:** Label above input. 1px border stroke. Focus ring: 2px accent color offset 2px. Error text below in semantic red. No floating labels.
- **Navigation:** Primary surface background. Active item: accent color indicator. Font weight 500 when active.
- **Skeletons:** Shimmer animation matching component dimensions. No circular spinners.
- **Empty States:** Icon-based composition with descriptive text and action button.
